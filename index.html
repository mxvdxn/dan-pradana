01 / PRACTICE

What the work involves.

A film is only the finished piece. The harder part is making the characters, the world and the production process hold together over time. That is where much of Dan's work happens.

01 / SERIAL STORYTELLING

Micro-drama that keeps going

Writing and developing episodic stories with recurring characters, evolving relationships and a consistent tone, rather than treating every video as a one-off experiment.

02 / WORLDBUILDING

Characters with somewhere to live

Creating original IP with its own locations, histories and rules. The detail matters because each new scene has to feel like it belongs to the same world.

03 / VISUAL EXECUTION

Making it feel like a film

Directing generative shots with attention to natural performances, believable lighting, camera behavior and the small details that often give synthetic footage away.

04 / WORKFLOW DESIGN

Systems behind the scenes

Building repeatable workflows for references, prompts, shot planning and continuity, so a story can grow without starting from scratch every time.

PROJECT NOTES / THE DEAD MALL
CREATIVE CONTEXT

Dan has spent years working in advertising, where even an interesting idea needs a budget, a production plan and somebody willing to approve it. Generative filmmaking introduced a different way of working. He could write something unusual and begin exploring it for himself.

WE FOUND SOMETHING IN... [THE DEAD MALL] follows a fictional gang of scooter-riding girls, loosely inspired by the Indonesian cabe-cabean reference, into an abandoned shopping mall at 2 a.m. Once inside, they discover something that should not be there. Rather than leave, they get drawn further into trouble.

The appeal was the freedom to mix local youth culture with absurd comedy and horror without sanding off the idea's rougher edges. Created with Seedance 2.0 through Runway, the film found a wide audience on X. The post analytics are listed above.

What matters to Dan is that these tools make room for stories he would otherwise have struggled to produce independently. The idea still has to be worth watching.

PROJECT NOTES / HIGH SCHOOL NO SAINTS
CREATIVE CONTEXT

With HIGH SCHOOL NO SAINTS, Dan uses generative filmmaking to explore a familiar setting from a sharper angle. Instead of a conventional coming-of-age scene, the story looks at the unwritten rules of status, loyalty and power among a fictional group of high school girls.

It draws on recognizable Indonesian school culture while pushing the attitude and tension toward something more heightened. The intention was not to produce a polished advertisement or make the characters agreeable. It was to see how far a distinct point of view could go in a short scene.

Made with Seedance 2.0, the post reached an audience on Instagram well beyond the initial experiment. The performance data is listed above. As with The Dead Mall, the more interesting outcome is the room these tools give Dan to develop ideas on his own terms.

04 / HOW IT'S MADE

Good stories need a working process.

Anyone can generate an interesting image. Sustaining a character, a setting and a believable performance across multiple scenes is a different problem. Dan's work combines writing with a growing set of practical production workflows.

This section leaves room for more detailed breakdowns of how those workflows are built and used.

WORKFLOW / FIELD NOTESOVERVIEW / 01
INPUT 01

Start with the story

Write the scene, the character intent and the visual logic before deciding which tool should make the shot.

SYSTEM 02

Build reference and continuity rules

Keep track of faces, wardrobe, locations, scale, lighting and camera direction so successive shots belong together.

OUTPUT 03

Refine what the audience sees

Pay attention to performance, motion, pacing and the little mistakes that can interrupt an otherwise convincing scene.

06 / BACKGROUND

A writer who kept finding new ways to make things.

Dan Pradana is a Jakarta-based copywriter and independent storyteller. He began his career in advertising agencies and now works on the corporate side of a global payments technology company.

Outside his day job, he runs MXVDXN Studio, where he writes and develops original projects including SARI & PLOY, 9 NAILS and BETWEEN DAYS. Generative filmmaking has given him a way to put those stories on screen without needing a conventional production setup for every idea.

His interests increasingly sit at the intersection of writing, visual direction and creative technology. He also participates in creative partnership programs with AI platforms, using the opportunity to test emerging tools in real storytelling projects.

He isn't interested in technology as a substitute for having something to say. He is interested in what it makes possible when the story is already there.
